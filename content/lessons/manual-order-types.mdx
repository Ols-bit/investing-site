---
title: Market orders and limit orders
description: Compare an order to trade now with an order that sets a price boundary.
---

An order tells a broker what you want to trade and under what conditions. A <Term>market order</Term> prioritises execution at the best available prices, but the final price is not guaranteed. A <Term>limit order</Term> sets the highest price you will pay to buy, or the lowest price you will accept to sell, but it may not execute.

## Choosing an instruction

A market order can be useful to understand when speed matters more than an exact price. In a fast-moving or thinly traded market, however, the execution price can differ from the last displayed price. A limit order sets a boundary, but the market may never reach it. Order features and names can vary between brokers.

## Worked example

A share is quoted around £30. You enter a buy limit order at £29.50 for 10 shares. It will only execute at £29.50 or less, if sellers are available. If the price stays above £29.50, you may end up with no shares. A market order would instead seek execution at available prices, which might be £30.10 or another price by the time the order arrives.
