<Definition term="Stocks and Shares ISA">
A <Term>Stocks and Shares ISA</Term> is a UK account that can hold eligible investments. It is a <Highlight color="blue">tax wrapper</Highlight>: income and gains inside the ISA are generally free from UK tax, but the value of investments can still fall.
</Definition>

<KeyIdea>
An ISA changes how eligible investments are taxed. It does not change the investment's risk or guarantee a return.
</KeyIdea>

## How it works

The ISA allowance is a limit on how much you can pay into eligible ISAs during a tax year. You can split it between permitted ISA types, subject to the rules for each.

The Lifetime ISA has its own contribution limit within the overall annual ISA allowance.

An ISA does not make an investment safer, guarantee a return, or remove charges.

You generally cannot move ordinary shares you already own directly into an ISA; check the current transfer rules before acting.

<Watch>
Tax advantages do not prevent an investment from falling in value, and rules can change. Check current GOV.UK guidance for your circumstances.
</Watch>
