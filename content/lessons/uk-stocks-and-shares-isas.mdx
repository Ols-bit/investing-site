A <Term>Stocks and Shares ISA</Term> is a UK account that can hold eligible investments. It is a tax wrapper: income and gains inside the ISA are generally free from UK tax, but the value of investments can still fall.

## How it works

The ISA allowance is a limit on how much you can pay into eligible ISAs during a tax year. You can split it between permitted ISA types, subject to the rules for each. The Lifetime ISA has its own contribution limit within the overall annual ISA allowance.

An ISA does not make an investment safer, guarantee a return, or remove charges. You generally cannot move ordinary shares you already own directly into an ISA; check the current transfer rules before acting.

## Worked example

For the {ukFigures.taxYear} tax year, the annual ISA allowance is £{formatUKPounds(ukFigures.isaAnnualAllowance)}. If someone contributes £{formatUKPounds(ukFigures.examples.isaContribution)} to eligible ISAs, the arithmetic leaves £{formatUKPounds(ukExamples.isaRemainingAllowance)} of that allowance unused. This is an illustration of the limit, not a suggestion about how much to contribute.

## Rules can change

Tax rules can change and may depend on your circumstances. Check current GOV.UK guidance before making a decision. This lesson is educational and is not personal advice.
