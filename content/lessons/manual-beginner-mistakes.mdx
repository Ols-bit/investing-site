Common mistakes include investing money needed soon, buying because of a headline, trading too often, concentrating in one company, and assuming past gains will continue. A pause and a written reason for each decision can help separate a plan from a momentary reaction.

## Make decisions with a process

Before acting, ask what the investment is, what could make it lose value, when you might need the money, and what it costs. Avoid treating a price chart, social post or confident prediction as a guarantee. If the decision depends on tax or personal financial circumstances, seek qualified local guidance.

## Worked example

You have £600 set aside for a bill due in three months. A share has risen 20% this month, and you consider investing the £600 after seeing a headline. If it then falls 15%, the investment would be worth £600 × 0.85 = £510 just when the bill is due. A recent rise does not protect money from a future fall.
