UK tax can depend on the type of income, account, asset, tax band and your personal circumstances. A <Term>capital gain</Term> is generally the profit when an asset is sold for more than its allowable cost. <Term>Dividend</Term> income is a distribution a company may choose to pay to shareholders.

## Allowances are not investment protection

Outside tax-advantaged accounts, the <Term>annual exempt amount</Term> for an individual's capital gains is £{formatUKPounds(ukFigures.capitalGainsAnnualExemptAmount)} for the {ukFigures.taxYear} tax year. The <Term>dividend allowance</Term> is £{formatUKPounds(ukFigures.dividendAllowance)}. Income or gains above relevant allowances may be taxable; the rate and reporting rules depend on circumstances. Gains and dividends within an ISA are generally not subject to UK income tax or capital gains tax.

## Worked example

If an illustrative taxable gain is £{formatUKPounds(ukFigures.examples.taxableGainBeforeAllowance)}, subtracting the annual exempt amount leaves £{formatUKPounds(ukExamples.gainAfterAnnualExemptAmount)} above that allowance. If illustrative dividend income is £{formatUKPounds(ukFigures.examples.dividendsReceived)}, £{formatUKPounds(ukExamples.dividendsAboveAllowance)} is above the dividend allowance. These are arithmetic examples, not tax calculations: losses, other income, eligibility and reporting rules can change the result.

## Rules can change

Tax rules and figures can change each tax year. Check current GOV.UK information or consult a qualified tax professional. This lesson is educational and is not personal advice.
