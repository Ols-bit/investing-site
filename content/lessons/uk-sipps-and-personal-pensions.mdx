A <Term>personal pension</Term> is a way to save for retirement outside a workplace pension. A <Term>SIPP</Term> is a self-invested personal pension: it can offer a wider choice of investments, but choosing investments and understanding their risks remains important.

## Tax relief and limits

Eligible pension contributions can receive <Term>pension tax relief</Term> under the rules for the scheme and the person contributing. With relief at source, a provider claims basic-rate relief and adds it to the pension; some people may need to claim additional relief themselves. Tax relief is not the same as an employer contribution, and it does not mean the investment itself is risk-free.

For the {ukFigures.taxYear} tax year, the standard <Term>pension annual allowance</Term> is £{formatUKPounds(ukFigures.pensionAnnualAllowance)} across relevant pension saving, but it can be lower for some people. People with no relevant earnings may still qualify for relief on contributions up to a gross £{formatUKPounds(ukFigures.pensionGrossLimitWithoutEarnings)} under current rules.

## Worked example

For a simple relief-at-source illustration, a net contribution of £{formatUKPounds(ukFigures.examples.pensionNetContribution)} with relief at {ukFigures.pensionReliefAtSourceBasicRatePercent}% would receive £{formatUKPounds(ukExamples.pensionTaxRelief)} of basic-rate relief, giving £{formatUKPounds(ukFigures.examples.pensionNetContribution + ukExamples.pensionTaxRelief)} in the pension. This example is not a personal tax calculation. The treatment depends on eligibility, earnings and scheme rules.

## Rules can change

Pension tax treatment and access rules can change and may depend on your circumstances. Check current GOV.UK guidance or speak to a qualified adviser. This lesson is educational and is not personal advice.
