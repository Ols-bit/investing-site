<Definition term="Stock market">
The <Term>stock market</Term> is where investors can buy and sell shares. A <Term>broker</Term> provides access to a market and sends orders on a customer's behalf. Buyers and sellers meet through an <Term>exchange</Term> or another trading venue; prices change as their orders interact.
</Definition>

## Buyers, sellers and prices

An order to buy has a price and a quantity. An order to sell also has a price and quantity.

When compatible orders meet, a trade can take place. The latest trade price is a record of that match, not a promise that the next trade will happen at the same price.

Markets are open at set times, and availability differs by exchange. News, company results, economic conditions and many other factors can affect the prices people are willing to pay.

A broker may charge fees, and the price available when an order reaches the market can differ from a displayed quote.

<KeyIdea>
A displayed quote is information about prices now, not a promise about the next trade.
</KeyIdea>

## Worked example

<Example>
Imagine a buyer is willing to pay £24.90 for a share, while the lowest seller asks £25.10. No trade happens at those prices. If a buyer raises their offer to £25.10, the orders can match and a trade may take place at £25.10. This single trade does not establish what the share is worth in every context.
</Example>

<Steps>
  <li>The buyer offers £24.90 while the seller asks £25.10, so the orders do not match.</li>
  <li>If the buyer's offer rises to £25.10, the prices can match.</li>
  <li>A trade may then take place at that price; it is not a forecast of the next price.</li>
</Steps>
