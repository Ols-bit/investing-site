A <Term>workplace pension</Term> is a pension arranged through an employer. Under <Term>auto-enrolment</Term>, eligible workers are usually enrolled into a pension scheme, with contributions from the worker and employer under current rules.

## How contributions work

In most automatic-enrolment schemes, minimum contributions are calculated using a band of <Term>qualifying earnings</Term>. For the {ukFigures.taxYear} tax year, the band runs from £{formatUKPounds(ukFigures.workplaceQualifyingEarningsLower)} to £{formatUKPounds(ukFigures.workplaceQualifyingEarningsUpper)} a year. The usual minimum is {ukFigures.workplaceEmployeeMinimumPercent}% from the worker and {ukFigures.workplaceEmployerMinimumPercent}% from the employer, making {ukFigures.workplaceCombinedMinimumPercent}% in total. Scheme rules can result in different contribution calculations.

Your payslip, pension scheme and employer information explain how contributions are actually calculated. Leaving a scheme may mean missing employer contributions; consider getting guidance before making a change.

## Worked example

If £{formatUKPounds(ukFigures.examples.workplaceQualifyingPay)} is eligible pay for an example calculation, a {ukFigures.workplaceEmployeeMinimumPercent}% worker contribution is £{formatUKPounds(ukExamples.workplaceEmployeeContribution)} and a {ukFigures.workplaceEmployerMinimumPercent}% employer contribution is £{formatUKPounds(ukExamples.workplaceEmployerContribution)}. Together, £{formatUKPounds(ukExamples.workplaceEmployeeContribution + ukExamples.workplaceEmployerContribution)} goes into the pension before any separate tax-relief treatment. Real payroll calculations depend on scheme rules and earnings.

## Rules can change

Eligibility and contribution rules can change. Check current GOV.UK guidance and your scheme documents. This lesson is educational and is not personal advice.
