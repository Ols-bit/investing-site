## Why investing can matter

Investing means putting money into assets that <Highlight color="citron">may grow in value or pay income over time</Highlight>. Common examples include shares and bonds.

Their value can also fall, so investment growth is never guaranteed.

Saving and investing can serve different purposes. Savings are generally easier to access and their value is more predictable.

Investments can offer the possibility of long-term growth, but they involve the risk of losing some or all of the money invested.

<Watch>
Investing is not a guaranteed shortcut to growth. The value can fall, and the money may not be available at the time or value you expect.
</Watch>

## The role of time and compounding

<Definition term="Compounding">
Compounding is when returns build on earlier returns. Over a long period, this can contribute to growth, but investment returns vary and may be negative. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
</Definition>

Investing also involves uncertainty. Prices can change because of company performance, economic conditions, and many other factors.

Spreading money across different investments can reduce the effect of any one investment performing poorly, but it cannot remove risk.
