Diversification means spreading investments across different companies, industries or asset types. It can reduce the effect of one holding performing poorly, but it cannot remove market risk or prevent losses. A portfolio holding many similar companies may be less diversified than it appears.

## Spread exposure thoughtfully

Different investments can respond differently to the same events. Diversification is about how holdings relate to one another, not simply how many names appear on a list. Broad funds can provide exposure to many holdings, but their values can still fall and they have their own costs and risks.

## Worked example

Portfolio A puts £1,000 into one company. Portfolio B splits £1,000 equally across 10 unrelated companies. If the first company falls 30% while the other nine stay unchanged, Portfolio A loses £300. Portfolio B loses £30, or 3% of its original value. If all 10 fall together, diversification will not prevent a loss.
