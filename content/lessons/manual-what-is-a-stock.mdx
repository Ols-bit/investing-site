A <Term>Stock</Term>, also called a <Term>share</Term>, represents a small ownership interest in a company. Owning a share does not give you control of the business, but it can give you a claim on its future value. Share prices can rise or fall, and a company can lose value.

## What owning a share can mean

Companies can issue shares to raise money. Investors may later trade those shares with one another. Some companies may pay dividends, which are distributions of company money, but dividends are not guaranteed. Shareholders can also lose some or all of the money they invest.

## Worked example

Suppose a company has 1,000,000 shares and you own 10. You own 10 / 1,000,000, or 0.001% of its shares. If a share costs £20, your 10 shares are worth £200 before fees and taxes. If the price falls to £15, their value becomes £150; the ownership percentage is the same, but the value has fallen by £50.
