A stock chart plots a price over time. The horizontal axis shows time and the vertical axis shows price, though chart settings can change the scale. A line chart often uses closing prices; candlestick charts show opening, closing, high and low prices for each interval.

## Use the time range as context

A one-day chart can look very different from a five-year chart. Check the period and scale before comparing movements. A chart describes past prices; it cannot show what will happen next. Short movements are noisy, and a visual pattern is not proof of a reliable forecast.

## Worked example

On a daily candlestick chart, a share opens at £50, reaches a high of £53, falls to a low of £48, and closes at £51. The candle covers a £5 range. Since the close is above the open, the period ended higher than it began, even though the price moved both up and down during the day.
